## America's Tapestry Opens at the Muscarelle!

It was an unforgettable afternoon of needlework and community as we gathered for the first time to enjoy the opening of the America's Tapestry exhibition at the [Muscarelle Museum of Art](https://muscarelle.wm.edu/exhibition/americas-tapestry-13-colonies-13-stories-1-nation/) in beautiful Williamsburg, Virginia. **Nearly 2,000 hands and 30,000 hours of embroidery went into these panels!**

![A visitor reads about the New Jersey panel of America's Tapestry](/images/news/2026-07/01.jpg)

*A visitor reads about the New Jersey panel of America's Tapestry*

The day began with a **Stitcher's Showcase** where fiber arts enthusiasts had the opportunity to meet our state representatives and learn from the team behind each panel. 10 of our 13 participating states were able to be represented, allowing for many of our volunteers to meet one another for the first time!

At 4 PM, the museum hosted a panel discussion with Karen Wallach (SC), Jenni Paperman (NY), Mary Van Tyne (PA), and Catherine Theron (VA), who discussed how each of their states approached this monumental project.

![Virginia Director Catherine Theron shares her insights during the panel discussion](/images/news/2026-07/04.jpg)

To cap off the day, a reception and gallery viewing were hosted by the museum. We were astounded by the sheer number of well-wishers who came to support us. A huge thank you to both the museum staff and our volunteers who made the day truly special. **Several of our attendees traveled over 700 miles to share in this moment with us. We thank you!**

## Stitcher Spotlight: Meet Anne Hart-Davies!

![An embroidered object by Anne Hart-Davies](/images/news/2026-07/20.jpg)

***An Embroidered Object by Anne Hart-Davies***

**Anne Hart-Davies** is an embroiderer on the Massachusetts tapestry panel. A resident of Kingston, Massachusetts, she has been stitching for more than fifty years, having learned from her mother — an avid stitcher who set her to work on her first crewel piece at age fifteen. Before America's Tapestry she practiced beading, cross stitch, canvaswork, and surface embroidery including crewel and raised work.

On the panel she has worked in French knots, feather stitch, leaf stitch, and Bayeux stitch, with the French knot her favorite, and she picked up the Bayeux stitch as a new technique along the way. Though she came to Massachusetts from New York in 1979, she considers herself a Massachusetts native.

She was drawn to the project by the chance to contribute to an important piece of the nation's future history and to promote the art of stitching, and she came away having learned about the importance of cocoa to the soldiers and the Colony. Her hope for the 250th is that Americans can come together as one country to celebrate the milestone.

***Thank you for donating your time and talent, Anne!***
